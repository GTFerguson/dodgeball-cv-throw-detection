throw efficiency = eliminations ÷ throws
per team, per set. Fakes and passes don't count

Why this one

  • The rulebook only sees the ball. A fake is the same arm as a throw, so the body alone can't decide. You have to find the ball.
  • It's dense and it's hard. 60 attempts in one set: 29 throws, 25 fakes, 6 passes. The ball is 20 pixels, there are six of them, and the hit itself usually can't be seen.

The metric

Dodgeball's shooting percentage. How often a throw takes someone off the court. Per team, per set, and per player once identity holds.

Ground truth

One set labelled by hand with a purpose-built tool: 60 events, anchored on the release frame, thrower and target clicked, ambiguous cases flagged. 3 flagged uncertain, every flag with a note.

Pose finds the arm. Colour finds the ball. The game tells us the outcome.

Three decisions that shaped it

  • The body can't tell a fake from a throw. Every pose feature we tried was a coin flip. So we look for the ball at the wrist and watch it leave.
  • Pass or throw: where the ball went, not where it was aimed. Follow it until it reaches someone, or check which way it's heading.
  • The count says someone left. The ball says whose. When a body leaves the court a throw gets the credit. To pick which, we follow the ball through the player it reached: a ball that comes back off him struck him, a ball that carries on didn't. A ball that turned and put nobody out was blocked.
One frame as the pipeline sees it: the court fit in green, a skeleton on every body, and the jersey numbers it read. Players are stitched by number and side, not track id.

When the number's wrong, it's one of three things

The system got it wrong

±25 points

Mostly the last step. Who gets credit for a walk-off now comes from the ball's bounce, and the bounce threshold was set by eye on this clip from six hits. The ball is found by colour, and colour is lost against the red team at the far end.

The label was wrong

±4 points

I labelled the set myself. Three calls I wasn't sure of are flagged; if all three went the other way, efficiency moves four points.

Nobody can tell

no frame

At 25 fps and 20 px there is no frame where the ball touches anyone. A clean hit bounces off and the ball shows it; a graze barely bends the flight and looks like a miss. Below is one.

Hit, or a narrow miss?

My note on it says "could be a super narrow miss or a hit". Nobody left the court, so it went down as a miss. That's the game deciding, not the picture.

And with fifteen throws a side, one set says little about a team either way. The metric wants a match.

The rule

Someone walks off; two throws could have done it. Before: whoever threw last. Now: whoever's ball came back off him. A ball that carries on didn't hit anyone. A ball that came back and put nobody out was blocked.

Fixed 5 of the 9 wrong: this pair, two blocks the count could never see, and a hit on a player already walking off.

How

A segmenter (SAM 2) is shown the ball on one frame and finds it again after it disappears behind the body. The colour tracker couldn't: it predicts where the ball is going next, and a hit breaks that by definition.

The bounce threshold was set by eye on this clip from six hits. That's the whole sample; slide 10.

What the count saw

In between, at 1:48.0, 18 hits far 2 as he walks off: already out, so it moves nobody. Three things on one player in two seconds.

Two USA players walk off. Two Canada players should walk on; the roster registered one. A walk-off with nobody coming back is a hit by the count's rules, so the first catch is scored a miss and the hit lands on a throw that came later.

Why the ball can't help here

A caught ball just stops in the hands. Its flight barely bends, same as a ball that sails past. The bounce tells hits from misses; it says nothing about catches.

Whose fault

Identity. Tracks fragment when players cross the sideline, and the second returning player was never stitched back to a number. The fix is upstream of everything on the previous slide.
